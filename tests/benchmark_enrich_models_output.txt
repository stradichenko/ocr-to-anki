Enrichment: Gemma 4 E2B against Gemma 3 4B
=========================================

Produced by tests/benchmark_enrich.py with --text-only, on the target laptop
(Intel Gen9.5 iGPU, Vulkan, llama.cpp b11577), twelve Japanese words in two
batches of six, identical prompts for both models.

The question this answers is whether moving the default to Gemma 4 E2B was
right. It had been moved on licence and architecture grounds without ever
checking the output.

First run, before the parser was fixed:

    Gemma 4 E2B    3 of 12 usable   10.47s per word
    Gemma 3 4B    12 of 12 usable   15.95s per word

That looked damning, and it was wrong. Reading what the model actually returned
showed it labels only the first entry:

    WORD: 探す
    DEF: To search for something that is lost or unknown.
    - 静か
    DEF: Quiet and free from noise or activity.

The parser split on WORD:, so every later entry was swallowed into the first,
and the last DEF: in a block won - the first word was given the last word's
definition, silently. Separately, the UNKNOWN marker was matched as a substring,
so the definition above, which contains the word "unknown", was discarded as a
miss despite being a correct answer.

With both fixed:

    Gemma 4 E2B   12 of 12 usable   11.86s per word   prompt eval 3354ms/word
    Gemma 3 4B    12 of 12 usable   15.99s per word   prompt eval 4376ms/word

Both produce a usable result for every word. Gemma 4 is about 26% faster per
word and spends less on prompt evaluation, which is consistent with being a
smaller model.

So the move to Gemma 4 was right. It was nearly reversed on the strength of a
bug in the code reading its output, which is worth recording: the parser was
the thing that was worse, not the model.

Caveat: twelve words, one word list, one machine. Enough to show the earlier
conclusion was an artifact and to justify the default, not enough to rank the
two models in general.
