Vision image-token sweep
========================

Produced by tests/benchmark_vision_tokens.py, which runs llama-mtmd-cli at
several --image-max-tokens budgets over the same image and reports encode cost
against output quality, with the flag omitted as the control.

Conditions:
  host     gary, Intel UHD Graphics CML GT2 (Gen9.5), the project's target
  runtime  llama.cpp b11577 prebuilt, the asset the Linux build downloads
  model    gemma-4-e2b-it-q4_0.gguf + mmproj, Vulkan backend
  image    a handwritten French vocabulary page, downscaled to 768px as the
           app does before OCR
  quality  the reference words amour, bouleaux and pencha, taken from the word
           list this repository recorded with its OpenCL build

Why this matters: the repository originally measured the vision encode at
roughly 104 seconds on OpenCL, more than half of a whole OCR pass. The budget
turns out to control it directly.

Measured encode, with the flag applied:

  budget 560:  12,493 ms
  budget  70:   3,475 ms

A 3.6x reduction against identical output (28 lines either way, same expected
words found). Against the original 104 s OpenCL figure, budget 70 is a ~30x
improvement in encode time, most of which comes from moving to the Vulkan build
on the current runtime and the rest from the budget.

Output quality did not degrade across the sweep. The answer text stayed at
1447-1462 characters from unset through budget 280, and the expected-word count
was flat at 2/3, rising to 3/3 at budget 140. The variation is word recognition
noise, not a budget effect.

One caveat visible in both runs: the model still emits a reasoning channel even
with the system prompt, on this dense a page. It is stripped before the word
list is assembled, and that stripping was validated against this captured
output with no narration leaking through.
